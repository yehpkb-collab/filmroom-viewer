Film Room

Put timed notes and emoji right on a YouTube video, then send a link so someone else sees them too. Built for coaching and scouting. Setup takes about two minutes.

You need a laptop or desktop with the Brave or Chrome browser (Windows, Mac or Linux). It does not run on phones or tablets. This is an early test version, so tell me about anything that breaks or confuses you.

Install it

Download Film Room v0.6a · 33 KB

  1. Unzip it

    Open the downloaded file and extract it. On Windows, right-click it and choose Extract All. On a Mac, double-click it. You get a folder called film-room-overlay.

    Move that folder somewhere it can stay, like Documents. Your browser runs Film Room from that folder, so don’t delete it.

  2. Open your browser’s extensions page

    Open a new tab, paste this into the address bar, and press Enter:

    brave://extensions

    On Chrome, use chrome://extensions instead.

    Then turn on Developer mode (the switch at the top right of that page).

  3. Load it

    Click Load unpacked, pick the film-room-overlay folder (the one with a file named manifest.json inside), and confirm.

Check that it worked: open any YouTube video (reload the tab if it was already open). A small “Film Room” tag appears at the top right of the video. Chrome may show a “disable developer mode extensions” message when it starts. That is normal for extensions installed this way, so you can close it.

Make notes

With a YouTube video open, use these keys (they don’t work while you’re typing in a text box). On a Mac, Alt is the Option key.

Alt + NAdd a note at this moment. Type it, pick an emoji if you like, press Enter. Notes start about 2 seconds before you pressed, so the viewer sees it coming.
Alt + BQuick bookmark with one keypress. For the next 8 seconds, press 1 to 8 to change its emoji.
Alt + EEnd the note that is on screen right now.
Alt + LNotes list on the video. Click a row to jump there; the pencil edits it.
Alt + PNotes panel in YouTube’s right-hand column.
Alt + ,  Alt + .Previous / next note.

What the emoji mean

  • 👀 Watch this
  • 🎯 Target or placement
  • 💡 Tip or idea
  • ✅ Good execution
  • ⭐ Great, copy this
  • ❌ Poor execution
  • ⚠️ Risky choice
  • 🤔 Why? Discuss this

On the video

  • Drag a note to move it. Drag its bottom-right corner to reshape it. Double-click it to put it back.
  • The thin bar along the bottom of a note shows how much time it has left on screen.
  • Everything you write stays in your own browser, for that video. Nothing is uploaded until you share.

A good way to work

  • Watch the video once with Alt + B, dropping bookmarks. Go back afterwards and add words where it matters.
  • Keep notes to a line. One idea per note.

Share your notes

Press Alt + S (or Copy link in the notes list). Film Room copies a short link. Paste it into a text or email.

What the other person sees

  • They have Film Room: the link opens the video on YouTube with your notes drawn on top of it.
  • They don’t: it opens a simple page with the video and your notes beside it. They should watch it on that page. If they tap through to YouTube, they get the plain video without the notes.

Good to know

  • Anyone with the link can read the notes. Don’t put anything private in notes you share.
  • Sharing stores only the video, its title, your notes and a random ID that isn’t tied to you. No account, no email.
  • A link stops working after 90 days without anyone opening it. Add more notes, and sharing again makes a new link.
  • If you open your own link, Film Room tells you so and doesn’t duplicate your notes.

If something doesn’t work

  • Nothing shows up: reload the YouTube tab. Notes are hidden during ads, and Film Room only runs on youtube.com watch pages.
  • A key does nothing: make sure you’re not typing in a text box, and try clicking an empty part of the page first. If another extension uses the same key, it may win.
  • Updates: when I send a new zip, unzip it over the same folder, then click the reload arrow on Film Room in the extensions page.
